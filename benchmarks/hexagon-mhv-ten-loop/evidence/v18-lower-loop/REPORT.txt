V18: validated final-seam optimization at eight and nine loops
October 9, 2026, Pacific time

V18 is implemented in a separate package. It reproduces the known eight- and
nine-loop symbols in every checked coordinate. The final-seam phase is much
faster, while complete-prime savings are smaller because discovery and OPE
still dominate. The original V17 producer, data, checkpoints and accepted
ten-loop result remain unchanged. V18 has not been selected for ten-loop work.

What changed

1. Contract factors directly into candidate coordinates. V17 constructs each
   complete sparse source equation, sorts and merges it, then contracts with
   the transposed candidate. V18 sums the same factor products directly into
   candidate coordinates. Distributivity makes the two contractions identical
   over the finite field; no approximate arithmetic or sampled seam is used.

2. Continue the learning scan after an affine restriction. A later restriction
   is a left linear transformation of the current packed directions and origin.
   A previously satisfied row remains zero under that transformation. V18
   therefore continues at the next source block instead of reconstructing the
   satisfied prefix. A complete final sweep still verifies the resulting object.

3. Persist independent source-row addresses from the first prime. Later primes
   regenerate the partial seam and every selected residual coefficient/RHS,
   solve that small affine system, and lift once. No modular values are reused.
   Plans bind input identity, source/candidate dimensions and expected rank;
   checksum, identity, address and rank changes stop publication. Separate plans
   are learned when the incoming candidate dimension changes. Plans are published
   through synchronized temporary files, renames and a parent-directory sync.

The actual second nine-loop prime reused all 380 selected rows. Its small solve
took 0.0397 s, followed by 3.3369 s for complete seam verification. It performed
380 selected contractions and one lift. The learning prime instead contracted
372,388 rows and performed six lifts, then verified all rows.

Both routes retain one common affine origin for all six D3 sectors, the original
CG convention, the E metric (1,3), physical-copy-primitive normalization and
origin-last cache convention. V17's augmented discovery, early physical work
cursor, triangular OPE storage, shared MRK RHS and bonus ansatz are unchanged.
The plan uses addresses in the recomputed factored row basis, not a claim that
the modular numerical basis is identical at all primes. Layout/rank checks and
the complete final operator check are essential to its acceptance policy.

The measured cache improvement

At nine loops each terminal OPE head occupies 876,573,360 bytes. Three auxiliary
F values require 2,629,720,080 bytes, exceeding the original 2 GiB budget. The
first V18 trial generated the terminal head four times, including one repeated
value during checking. A 3 GiB cache retains all three values.

Configuration           Terminal generations     All logged head-generation time
V17, 2 GiB                      4                         126.203 s
V18, 2 GiB                      4                         124.097 s
V18, 3 GiB                      3                          94.199 s

The larger cache removes about 29.9 s of logged OPE head work in this trial and
reproduces all 105,898 known native coefficients. Its complete-prime peak RSS
increased only about 34 MB relative to V18/2 GiB: the uncached third head already
had to exist temporarily. This observation is specific to the tested data and
cache lifetime; it is not a universal memory guarantee.

Correctness and scope

- Ten small affine cases at two primes match independent rational-origin and
  free-direction formulas. Four guards reject lost replay rank, inconsistent
  origin, wrong identity and a corrupted plan.
- Direct contraction agrees with original row assembly on 4,224 actual sampled
  rows at each loop, including the full incoming candidate coordinates.
- All phase variants match every known native coefficient: 38,989 at eight
  loops and 105,898 at nine loops. Each also verifies all 133,214 or 372,388
  factored sewing rows through the independent original implementation.
- Complete V17 and V18 first-prime runs reproduce the saved native results.
- New V18 calculations at primes 1152921504606847009 and 1152921504606847067
  pass all current fresh collinear/MRK/OPE checks. Exact split conversion to
  12+4 and 14+4 agrees with reduction of the saved rational references at both
  primes. This compares every coefficient, not just solution dimensions.
- The 3 GiB nine-loop configuration also matches every native reference
  coefficient and passes the same physical holdouts.

The new calculations are finite-field controls against exact saved references.
They are not new full QQ reconstructions, function-level constants, unrestricted
uniqueness proofs or a complete test of all physical constraints. The inherited
bonus assumptions and finite supplied physical coverage remain explicit.

Measurement conditions

All timed numerical jobs were serialized after the other chat's mapped/heap
benchmarks completed. Mathematical audits and builds before that gate overlapped
those earlier jobs; their resource figures are not speed comparisons. Timing
used a matched GCC 14 build/runtime and native heap for both solvers. The primary
nine-loop order was V18 then V17; the eight-loop order was V17 then V18. Source,
binary, command, log, input, plan and cache identities are retained.

These are warm-basis sewing measurements with precomputed FEC/LEC/physical
inputs. Compiler time, cold basis construction and data generation are excluded.
Each numerical job used 16 threads, 4 GiB physical projection cache, a decimal
18 GB cgroup pressure threshold and 20 GB hard memory cap, no swap, a 96 GiB
virtual address limit and a 3 GiB host-availability reserve. RSS was sampled at
100 ms; cgroup peaks are reported separately. All memory limit/OOM counters
were zero. Fast helper-process RSS samples can miss a sub-100-ms process.
The production allowance remains 50,000,000,000 bytes.

This establishes a tested optimization at these loops, not global solver
optimality. Complete-prime timing replication, full ten-loop V18 testing and
partial/discovery grouped-pivot replay remain unmeasured. Structural replay
currently covers final sewing only.

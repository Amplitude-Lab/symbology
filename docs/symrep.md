# Rational symmetry kernels and recursive FEC/LEC bases

`symrep` constructs a kernel basis as complete rational irreducible copies of
the finite group generated by supplied matrices. It uses the repository's
patched SparseRREF for exact kernels and FLINT for rational polynomial
factorization and integer lattice reduction. It does not have a parity, D3,
or D7 dispatch table.

Build with the same C++20 toolchain and libraries as `bootstrap`:

```bash
make symrep
make check-symrep
```

The recursive driver has two backends. `--backend multiplicity` solves only
primitive-corner variables, using cached equivariant contraction templates.
When all rational irreps are split, it also keeps the solved recurrence in
scalar multiplicity coordinates, without constructing component tensors.
`--backend factorized` keeps a sparse working basis and an exact irreducible
frame, avoiding repeated expansion into dense canonical coordinates. It uses
ordinary sparse elimination internally. The default `auto` chooses factorized
when a rational irrep has a non-scalar endomorphism algebra, and multiplicity
otherwise; when resuming it follows the saved chain format. This is a heuristic,
not a promise that one backend wins for every representation.

## What was learned from the hexagon archive

The inspected `hexagon_irr_bootstrap_v2_20260928.tar.gz` stores FEC and LEC
recurrences in parity × D3 multiplicity spaces. Its six sectors are
`pe_A1`, `pe_A2`, `pe_E`, `po_A1`, `po_A2`, and `po_E`.
The fixed Clebsch–Gordan maps in `src/reduced_d3/irr_recurrence.hpp` expand a
multiplicity vector into a whole irreducible copy. The constraint tensor is
adapted on its equation axis as well as its two letter axes; each extension
solves the resulting smaller blocks. Its rational two-dimensional E model
uses a nontrivial invariant metric, so transposes, dual actions and a common
normalization for both components matter.

The reusable idea is to keep the representation convention and tensor-product
intertwiners at every weight. The new implementation constructs these data from
the supplied matrices instead of copying the archive's fixed six-sector rules.
The archive's actual FEC and MHV LEC executables provide independent reference
chains; matching dimensions alone is not considered a sufficient check.

## Mathematical contract

Vectors and basis vectors are rows. A supplied generator acts by
`v -> v R(g)`. Constraints are `A v^T = 0`, and an adapted basis satisfies

```text
B R(g) = D(g) B,
D(g) = direct sum over s of (I_m_s tensor rho_s(g)).
```

The kernel must be invariant under every generator. For independent equation
rows, the solver derives their action and verifies exactly

```text
A R(g)^T = S(g)^T A.
```

If this fails, the requested decomposition of the entire kernel is not defined;
the CLI rejects the input. For example, a single arbitrary coordinate constraint
need not preserve a cyclic symmetry. Redundant equation rows are allowed and
are removed before deriving their action.

Over QQ, semisimplicity gives `V = direct sum_s U_s^(m_s)`. It does **not** always
give one scalar multiplicity matrix per complex irrep. Write
`E_s = End_G(U_s)` and `e_s = dim_QQ(E_s)`. A primitive group-algebra projector
has rank `e_s` on one copy of `U_s`. The multiplicity backend restricts to its image, solves
that kernel over QQ, and reconstructs whole copies using the fixed group orbit
of the projector. A corner nullity of `e_s * k` produces `k` full copies of
dimension `dim_QQ(U_s)`. It never diagonalizes noncommuting generators one at
a time.

For the supplied heptagon matrices, cyclic and flip generate D7 and parity is
an independent central involution. The selected group is therefore D7 × C2,
of order 28. Its rational irreps have dimensions `1,1,1,1,6,6`, with
endomorphism dimensions `1,1,1,1,3,3`. Each six-dimensional rational irrep keeps
three conjugate complex two-dimensional irreps together. Splitting those into
separate two-dimensional models would require extending QQ.

Symmetry-related equations are not necessarily redundant constraints on an
arbitrary vector: under coordinate exchange, `x1=0` generates `x2=0`, but the
second equation cannot simply be dropped while `x2` remains a free variable.
The reduction must act on the unknowns and equations together. The multiplicity backend solves
for the maps between equivalent irreducible copies; symmetry then generates
all components of each solved copy.

For split irreps (`e_s=1`), an equivariant block is a scalar multiplicity matrix
tensored with the identity on `U_s`. Only that multiplicity matrix needs a
kernel solve. For the six-dimensional heptagon irreps, each coefficient belongs
to a three-dimensional endomorphism field. The multiplicity backend represents it
as three rationals, so one candidate copy contributes three corner variables,
not six unrestricted component variables. Treating it as a single field
element would require a field-aware elimination backend. Simply discarding two
of its three rational coordinates would change the solution space.

## Standalone kernel and representation conversion

Inputs are exact rational SparseArray WXF matrices in the existing format.
The columns of `A` must match the dimension of every generator.

```bash
./symrep kernel --matrix constraints.wxf \
  --generator cyclic=data/cycrepmat.wxf \
  --generator flip=data/fliprepmat.wxf \
  --generator parity=data/parityrepmat.wxf \
  --output output_symrep/kernel --threads 2

./symrep adapt \
  --generator cyclic=data/cycrepmat.wxf \
  --generator flip=data/fliprepmat.wxf \
  --generator parity=data/parityrepmat.wxf \
  --output output_symrep/alphabet
```

`kernel_basis.wxf` contains the organized kernel in the original input
coordinates. `kernel_g0.wxf`, etc. are the generator actions on that kernel.
Rows are ordered **irrep, copy, component**. `kernel_copies.tsv` records those
boundaries. `source_basis.wxf` records the original representation's adaptation;
`kernel_adapted_coordinates.wxf` expresses the answer in that source basis.
The `adapt` command writes the analogous `space_*` files.

For a subsequent constraint matrix whose columns are the previous kernel
coefficients, reuse its bundle directly:

```bash
./symrep kernel --matrix next_constraints.wxf --input output_symrep/kernel \
  --output output_symrep/next-kernel
```

This preserves the original group and irrep labels, including if the new
representation is no longer faithful or the kernel becomes zero-dimensional.

In C++, `Group`, `Model` and `Layout` are reusable objects. Keep the same group
and models, and pass a returned solution layout to the next `kernel_adapted`
call. That entry point checks invariance before restricting to primitive
corners. The lower-level `kernel_blocks` provider interface assumes the caller
has already established equivariance; the recursive driver proves this from
the prepared input and its checked tensor intertwiners.
`TensorAdapter::product` constructs and caches the rational Clebsch–Gordan
changes for tensor products.

## Automatically prepare the heptagon data

No hand-written heptagon character table, letter permutation, equation-sector
table, or adapted dlog tensor is needed:

```bash
./symrep prepare \
  --generator cyclic=data/cycrepmat.wxf \
  --generator flip=data/fliprepmat.wxf \
  --generator parity=data/parityrepmat.wxf \
  --condition data/dlogmat_E6.wxf --seed data/FEC_1.wxf \
  --direction forward --output output_symrep/heptagon-fec-input --threads 2

./symrep extend --input output_symrep/heptagon-fec-input \
  --max-weight 4 --output output_symrep/heptagon-fec-w4 --threads 2
```

For LEC use `--seed data/LEC_1.wxf --direction backward` in a separate prepared
directory. Forward tensors have shape `{basis_w, basis_(w-1), letter}`;
backward tensors have shape `{basis_w, letter, basis_(w-1)}`.

Preparation adapts the alphabet, derives and adapts the independent equation
representation, transforms both letter axes and the dual equation axis of
the condition tensor, and adapts the seed. A scalar terminal is assumed only
when that seed axis has dimension one. Otherwise supply
`--terminal-generator NAME=FILE` for every generator, in the same order.
Vector terminals are covered by public regression tests.

If `B_L` and `B_E` are the letter and equation changes of basis, the transformed
flattened condition is

```text
D_new = B_E^(-T) D_independent (B_L tensor B_L)^T.
```

With `--backend multiplicity`, the source and equation tensor products are already
organized by the same rational models. The solver assembles primitive corner
blocks directly and calls SparseRREF on those blocks. For split rational models,
the solved scalar matrix together with the fixed coupling maps specifies every
component of every copy. Only these scalars are saved and used in the following
extension. Independent large blocks share the requested worker budget.

`RecurrenceTemplates` constructs a basis of
`Hom_G(U_b, U_a tensor alphabet)` from the primitive corner of the tensor
product (with reversed factors for LEC). An intertwiner is determined by the
image of the canonical cyclic vector. `EquivariantConditions` also reduces the
condition map: after coupling the two letter factors, each block between
equivalent copies is the transpose of a commuting endomorphism. Only its
`dim_QQ End_G(U_s)` rational coefficients are stored. Exact reconstruction of
the full transformed condition certifies this reduction during preparation.

The contraction templates combine these condition coefficients with cached
recoupling maps between individual irreps. Component contractions are confined
to those small irreps; alphabet-copy and equation-copy axes are never expanded
in template construction. Sparse routes combine the resulting coefficients
between copies. These templates depend on the fixed representations and
condition, not on the growing number of copies. All `dim_QQ End_G(U_b)` coordinates are retained;
this also works for number-field and quaternionic commutants. No D3-specific
scalar assumption is used.

The solver feeds its solved multiplicity coordinates directly into the next
step. For split models, full component tensors are generated only by `expand`
or optional comparison. Non-split multiplicity solves retain explicit components.
A resumed legacy chain can be compressed from its
saved tensor. Templates are cached within a process and rebuilt on continuation;
they are not yet persisted as a prepared-input cache.

For `dim_QQ End_G(U)=1`, independent corner kernel rows already give independent
whole copies. Their orbits are lifted without repeating elimination, and only
one primitive normalization is applied per copy. Lattice shortening is skipped
in this case: it can destroy sparsity and cost far more than the actual solve.

For higher-dimensional endomorphism algebras, integer lattice saturation and
LLL still shorten large rational corner bases in independent batches of at most
128 rows. Selection of independent whole copies uses the original kernel's
private free coordinates. Restriction to those coordinates is injective on the
kernel, so the rank calculation uses nullity columns rather than every ambient
candidate coordinate, including after lattice changes. The lattice transformation and its unimodular determinant
are checked exactly. Reconstruction scales a whole irreducible copy together;
independent component normalization would change its representation matrices.
For integral kernel rows `H`, a Hermite form `HNF(H^T) = [T; 0]` gives the
saturated rows by solving `T^T S = H`. Private free-coordinate columns certify
a modulus for FLINT's modular Hermite routine. The implementation solves the
triangular system directly, avoiding a large intermediate rational inverse.

## Sparse recursion with an irreducible frame

For `--backend factorized`, write the logical basis at weight `w` as
`B_w = H_w C_w`. Here `C_w` is a sparse basis in the original letter coordinates
and the preceding **carrier** basis. Rows of the square, invertible `H_w` are
ordered by irrep, copy, and component. Generator matrices `S_w(g)` act on the
carrier, and the frame satisfies exactly

```text
H_w S_w(g) = D_w(g) H_w.
```

The implementation does the following at each weight:

1. Contract the preceding sparse carrier with the original independent
   condition rows, assembling independent rows in parallel. Compute the full
   QQ kernel using SparseRREF and retain native primitive normalization.
2. Inherit generator actions directly modulo a good prime through the kernel's
   free-coordinate chart. Large rational action matrices are unnecessary for
   this selection step. Primes with bad denominators or singular charts are
   retried without repeating the QQ kernel solve.
3. Determine rational multiplicities from character traces. Where the small
   irrep models certify a suitable generator eigenspace, use one finite-field
   eigencomponent per rational copy. Share this eigenspace between sectors
   and split the remaining commuting symmetries only in that smaller space.
   The scalar and nonscalar selections share the requested worker budget.
4. Select and certify every required cyclic generator now. Store its carrier
   row and an optional rational group-algebra prefilter. A small matrix pencil
   repairs a singular initial pairing when an involution inverts the selected
   cyclic generator. No equations or rank decisions remain deferred in the
   output. The other components are specified by the model's fixed group words.
5. Save the sparse carrier, ordered copy layout and exact orbit recipe. Pass
   the carrier and its modular actions into the next contraction.

The recipe for one copy is the orbit of
`e_i (R(h) - a I) P_s`, with integer `a`, a specified group element `h`, and the
rational primitive projector `P_s`. The default `h=identity, a=0` gives
`e_i P_s`. All rows of a copy receive one common primitive integer
normalization. These operations specify the exact QQ frame `H_w`; writing
its individual entries is optional. The next solve does not expand LEC.

A nonzero determinant in one residue-field embedding of the commutative
endomorphism field certifies independence over that number field, and hence
independence of whole rational irreducible copies. The small models certify
which eigencomponent is selected and that the rational projector pairs with
it nontrivially. Character multiplicities and the total dimension certify
completeness. This is an exact independence certificate, not a floating-point
approximation. The model identities are checked in the group algebra.
`--compare` additionally materializes and checks the entire rational frame,
its intertwining equations, rank and kernel residuals.

These shortcuts are discovered and checked on the supplied group's models.
A fixed-corner selector remains available when the spectral shortcut does not
apply. Groups without a certified fixed-corner plan, including the tested
quaternionic case, retain the general exact frame construction.

This backend preserves a symmetry-organized **factorization** throughout the
recursion. Its internal unknowns and equations are not reduced to multiplicity
blocks. The speed improvement comes from preserving sparsity, avoiding lattice
shortening and dense canonical-coordinate contractions, and generating whole
copies from selected symmetry seeds. Use `--backend multiplicity` when the
reduced-unknown elimination strategy itself is required.

To obtain exact transformation matrices at every saved weight without
materializing the basis:

```bash
./symrep actions --input PREPARED --chain CHAIN --output ACTIONS --threads 2
```

This works for compact multiplicity, factorized and expanded symmetry chains.
The output `wN_gJ.wxf` acts on row coefficients in the **logical irreducible
basis**. `actions.tsv` identifies the generators and coordinate convention;
`wN_copies.tsv` gives the block layout. An optional `--max-weight N` limits
the export. The matrices follow directly from the saved copy counts:

```text
D_w(g) = direct sum over s of (I_m(w,s) tensor rho_s(g)).
```

The irrep matrices `rho_s` are fixed for the entire chain. At each weight only
the multiplicities change, so there is no additional linear solve for `D_w`.
For a factorized chain, the certificate is `H_w R_carrier,w(g) = D_w(g) H_w`.
For an explicit adapted recurrence tensor, the corresponding identity is
`T_w (D_(w-1)(g) tensor R_letter(g)) = D_w(g) T_w`, with the tensor factors
reversed for LEC and all factors expressed in the tensor's coordinates.

The C++ helper `apply_irrep_action(v, layout, models, element)` applies a group
element directly to a canonical sparse coefficient row without constructing
the repeated block matrix. It uses a small accumulator for one irrep copy at
a time. Arbitrary products of generators can be evaluated in the fixed small
models rather than by multiplying growing full-size matrices.

For coefficients stored in a factorized chain's sparse carrier coordinates,
use `--coordinates carrier` instead. This reconstructs the exact rational
actions sequentially. It uses private free-variable columns when available
and constructs only the required columns of the tensor-product action; a
general coordinate chart remains available otherwise. The two coordinate
conventions must not be mixed. Converting an existing carrier-coordinate
vector into irreducible coordinates is additional work and is not included
in the direct-block construction/application timings.

To materialize a factorized chain in the existing expanded format:

```bash
./symrep expand --input output_symrep/heptagon-fec-input \
  --chain output_symrep/heptagon-fec-w4 \
  --output output_symrep/heptagon-fec-expanded --threads 2
```

For FEC the expanded recurrence is
`H_w C_w (H_(w-1)^(-1) tensor B_L^(-1))`; for LEC reverse the two right-hand
factors. At weight one use the prepared terminal basis in place of `H_0`.
The exporter checks the prepared seed exactly, writes `wN.wxf` and layouts,
and records expansion costs separately. Expanded output can be consumed or
continued by the multiplicity backend. Expansion can be much more expensive
than retaining the factorization and is not included in factorized solve times.

## Saved data and continuation

A prepared directory contains:

| Files | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `group.tsv`, `generator_*.wxf`, `group_words.tsv` | Defining generators and generated finite group |
| `irreps.tsv`, `irrep_*_g*.wxf` | Rational characters, dimensions and canonical generator models |
| `central_idempotents.wxf`, `primitive_idempotents.wxf` | Certified decomposition projectors in group-word order |
| `alphabet_*`, `equations_*`, `seed_*`, `terminal_*` | Basis changes, inverse where needed, copy layouts and actions |
| `condition_independent_rows.wxf` | Independent original equation rows |
| `dlogmat.wxf`, `seed.wxf`, `bootstrap.tsv` | Adapted recursive input and direction |
| `dlogmat_multiplicity.wxf`, `dlogmat_reduced.tsv` | Independent condition coefficients and their format version |
| `dlogmat_pair_basis.wxf`, `dlogmat_pair_copies.tsv` | Coupling convention for the two letter factors |
| `checksums.tsv` | Completion marker and SHA-256 hashes |

Group element zero is the identity; row zero of `group_words.tsv` is a sentinel.
Every later word is its parent word followed by the indexed generator. Treat
bundles as immutable and keep additional notes and logs outside them.

Reduced condition rows are ordered by equation irrep and copy; columns by
letter-pair irrep, coupled copy and endomorphism coordinate. For irrep `s`,
let `L` be the RREF row basis of its primitive projector. The commuting matrix
`J_e` has row `i` equal to `L[e] * rho_s(orbit[i])`. Each condition block in
coupled coordinates is `sum_e coefficient[e] * transpose(J_e)`. In particular,
the format supports non-scalar number-field and quaternionic coefficients.
The loader checks the saved pair basis against the current coupling convention.
Legacy prepared bundles are accepted and compressed once at process startup.

A split multiplicity chain uses schema `symbology-symrep-chain-v4` and format
`multiplicity`. It stores the explicit seed `w1.wxf`, a `wN_copies.tsv` at
every weight, and `wN_multiplicity.wxf` for `N >= 2`. Matrix rows are ordered
by output irrep and copy; columns by candidate irrep and copy. A scalar entry
`h` represents the entire component block `h I_dimension`. The model projector
and orbit are checked once to certify this convention. Each kernel row receives
one common primitive integer normalization. The tensor-product coupling maps
are regenerated from the certified prepared models when needed; normal solving
only enumerates their copy/channel layout. The complete representation requires
both the chain and its prepared bundle. `symrep expand` exports the chain as v1;
`verify` and `extend --chain` accept it directly. Existing v1 weights are
preserved when a resumed chain appends compact weights.

Normal split-model solving streams each assembled sector directly into its QQ
kernel solve. Large sectors are scheduled first; each requested worker holds
at most one live constraint block, with a private elimination workspace. Other
workers may assemble or solve another sector at the same time. The result order
and rational normalization are independent of scheduling. The driver retains
recurrence coefficients only when another weight needs them.

For this overlapping pipeline, `timings.tsv` records `pipeline_s` as elapsed
assembly-plus-elimination time and `planning_s` as its serial template/mapping
setup. `assembly_work_s` and `rref_work_s` sum the durations of individual sector
tasks; they are work totals and must not be added to estimate elapsed time.
`shorten_s` records subsequent primitive normalization. The old `assembly_s`,
`rref_s` and `kernel_s` are `nan` during normal streamed solving, because their
separate elapsed times are no longer meaningful. With `--compare`, assembly and
elimination remain separate so those columns retain their previous meaning.
The joint pipeline measurement excludes the optional comparison work.

For non-split models, an explicit multiplicity chain (schema `symbology-symrep-chain-v1`) stores `wN.wxf`, `wN_copies.tsv`, and for `N >= 2` the candidate tensor
product basis and solved kernel in those candidate coordinates. It also writes
`wN_multiplicity.wxf`: rows are ordered by output irrep and copy; columns by
candidate irrep, candidate copy and primitive-corner basis row. The corner basis
is the RREF row basis of that irrep's primitive-projector matrix. Each compact
row specifies the cyclic vector of one output copy; applying the fixed model's
orbit reconstructs its remaining component rows. This compact representation
retains endomorphism coordinates, not just one scalar per candidate copy.
`chain.tsv`
binds it to the exact prepared bundle. `timings.tsv` separates assembly,
adaptation, kernel work, and validation. It additionally reports template setup
and compression (parts of assembly), and RREF, shortening and copy lifting
(parts of kernel work). Missing historical measurements in continued chains
are `nan`. The saved recurrence satisfies
`wN = wN_kernel * wN_candidate_basis` after flattening its last two axes.

A factorized chain uses schema `symbology-symrep-chain-v3` and format
`factorized-orbits`:

| Files | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `wN_carrier.wxf` | Sparse recurrence in original letter and previous carrier coordinates |
| `wN_orbits.tsv` | Complete certified generator selection and rational prefilter for each copy |
| `wN_frame.wxf` | Explicit frame at the seed, or for the general fallback |
| `w1_carrier_gJ.wxf` | Exact defining-generator actions at the seed |
| `carrier_direction.tsv` | Direction for recursively evaluating later carrier actions |
| `wN_copies.tsv` | Logical irrep/copy/component row boundaries |
| `chain.tsv`, `checksums.tsv` | Format, maximum weight, prepared-bundle binding and integrity |
| `timings.tsv` | Assembly, QQ kernel, modular action, frame-selection and optional check times; carrier nnz, stored frame nnz and orbit seed count |

Higher-weight rational generator matrices and explicit frames are reconstructed
only when a consumer requests them. Orbit recipe v2 records `irrep`, `copy`,
`carrier_row`, `pre_element`, and `pre_shift`; recipe v1's unit-row convention
remains readable. The legacy factorized chain v2 with explicit frames and
all generator matrices also remains readable and resumable. Expanded chain
v1 is unchanged. Resuming preserves earlier bases and can append compact
weights to an explicit-frame chain.

At the final requested weight, normal orbit-format solving writes and validates
the rational carrier immediately after deriving its modular generator actions,
then releases it before selecting the orbit recipe. This reduces simultaneous
exact and modular storage. A bad-prime retry reloads the exact saved carrier;
it never repeats the QQ constraint solve. The output is sealed only after orbit
selection succeeds, and the saved chain remains resumable as before. Optional
`--compare` retains the exact carrier for its additional checks.

These factors specify the complete irreducible basis. Retain both the chain
and its prepared bundle; the carrier alone is not irreducibly organized.

Continue a completed chain into a new directory:

```bash
./symrep extend --input output_symrep/heptagon-fec-input \
  --chain output_symrep/heptagon-fec-w4 --max-weight 5 \
  --output output_symrep/heptagon-fec-w5 --threads 2
```

Existing directories are never overwritten. Incomplete outputs have no
completion seal and cannot be resumed. Saved primitive projectors and model matrices are certified
again when loading, reusing their existing convention; a different convention is
rejected instead of silently mixing basis coordinates. Conversion tables and
the prepared directory must be retained with a chain.

## Exact validation and benchmark reproduction

`extend --compare --backend multiplicity` additionally checks every cached block against direct
component contraction, assembles the full constraints, verifies
equivariance, checks exact residuals, computes an ordinary kernel in the same
input coordinates, and checks equality of spans. It can be expensive on the
denser adapted heptagon matrices. Benchmark timings exclude this optional
comparison. For the factorized backend, `--compare` checks the full kernel
residual, the inherited carrier actions against the ambient tensor actions,
the complete frame intertwiners, and frame invertibility. Ordinary reference
chains remain an independent check of completeness and recursive coordinates.

`ordinary` runs the same SparseRREF backend and thread settings recursively in
the original input coordinates:

```bash
./symrep ordinary --condition data/dlogmat_E6.wxf --seed data/FEC_1.wxf \
  --direction forward --max-weight 4 --output output_symrep/ordinary-fec

./symrep verify --input output_symrep/heptagon-fec-input \
  --chain output_symrep/heptagon-fec-w4 --reference output_symrep/ordinary-fec \
  --max-weight 4 --output output_symrep/verified-fec
```

`verify` accepts expanded, factorized and compact multiplicity chains and computes an exact invertible change of basis at **every weight**, by
inductively substituting the preceding change and the alphabet change into
the recurrence. It checks equality of the complete symbol spaces without
expanding every symbol word. It writes the changes of basis for inspection.
References use filenames `w1.wxf`, `w2.wxf`, etc. and the original input
alphabet convention.

To compare with the archive, build its `bin/irr_fec` and `bin/irr_lec` according
to its instructions, then compile the exporter against its own headers:

```bash
HEXAGON=/path/to/unpacked/hexagon_irr_bootstrap
g++ -O2 -std=c++20 -I"$HEXAGON/src" -I"$HEXAGON/src/reduced_d3" \
  -I"$HEXAGON/src/SparseRREF" \
  bench/hexagon_symrep_reference.cpp -o bench/hexagon_symrep_reference \
  -lflint -lgmp -lmpfr -ltbb -lmimalloc

python3 bench/symrep_benchmark.py --hexagon "$HEXAGON" \
  --output output_symrep_benchmark --repeats 3 --threads 2
```

Add the same dependency include/library flags used to build the main project
when they are installed outside the compiler's default search paths.
The benchmark needs Python's standard library and GNU `/usr/bin/time`.
It runs fresh output directories sequentially, records process wall time and
peak RSS, records internal phase times, and verifies every run against ordinary
chains and both hexagon archive chains. Preparation is reported separately
because a prepared bundle can be reused. Timing only the reduced RREF would
hide setup, assembly, reconstruction and output costs.

For a comparison with the actual original `bootstrap.hpp` extension functions,
compile `bench/native_symrep_reference.cpp` with the same flags and libraries as
`symrep`. Then run `bench/symrep_optimization.py --help`. That audit compares
the native implementation, a preserved pre-optimization binary, the current
solver, and the unpacked archive, and verifies every chain against both native
and archive references. It uses larger hexagon weights so startup does not
dominate. The archive still has its own SparseRREF version and writes compact
multiplicity files; its time is an end-to-end implementation comparison, not an
isolated RREF comparison.

The current before/after audit uses `bench/symrep_orbits.py --previous PATH`.
It records median full-process time, peak resident memory in KiB, and output
bytes for each implementation, with five fresh sequential processes per case.
The ordinary baseline is rebuilt with the same reconstruction patches and I/O
helpers as the current symmetry solver, so shared improvements benefit both.
Optional exact comparisons and
expanded exports are separate processes. `bench/symrep_reduced_probe.cpp` can
dump reduced blocks and compare pivot strategies on identical matrices.
For a change that leaves the complete saved bases byte-identical, an audit can
use `--validated-bases EARLIER_AUDIT`: prepared checksums and every saved native
and symmetry basis file must match the previously exactly verified run. The
report records the earlier proof's path and SHA-256 instead of repeating the
expensive reference changes of basis. Omitting this option always runs them.

Output still receives an exact semantic WXF readback. The parser visitor checks
the tensor header, dimensions, CSR offsets, indices and every rational value
without retaining a token object or another rational tensor for each nonzero.
The final factorized carrier is written through a read-only tensor view of the
kernel matrix; intermediate weights retain the tensor needed by the recurrence.
The view preserves the existing serialized bytes and continuation format.

Shared SparseRREF improvements parallelize row-height calculation and initial
rational reconstruction on larger matrices. Small signed residues use the same
uniqueness bound as FLINT's rational reconstruction; other residues use FLINT,
and the existing height certificate and multi-prime fallback remain. Original
non-pivot rows are released after the first modular forward elimination, once
their contribution to the full input height bound has been recorded. Only the
original pivot rows are needed for the subsequent CRT solves.

`bench/symrep_modular_probe.cpp` measures prime size and sparse versus dense
modular eigenspace elimination independently. Its diagnostic backend choices do
not change the production solver or its certificate prime.

For the earlier native/multiplicity/factorized comparison, run
`python3 bench/symrep_carrier.py --help`. It benchmarks the original hexagon
weights and heptagon FEC through weights 4 and 5 and LEC through weights 3 and
4. Every repeated run is verified against the native chain. Hexagon outputs
are also checked against the unpacked package. Factorized export is timed and
verified separately.

The compact orbit audit is `python3 bench/symrep_orbits.py --help`. Build
`bench/native_symrep_reference.cpp` with the same compiler flags as `symrep`.
It alternates sequential native/symmetry runs, checks exact agreement at every
weight, checks repeated basis files, and compares both hexagon chains with the
unpacked archive. Reused preparation, optional checks and explicit expansion
are excluded from solve timings and must be reported separately.

The latest measurements and exactness argument are recorded in
[the compact-orbit audit](../audits/symrep-orbits-2026-10-02/REPORT.md).
It reports both the larger-chain near-parity and the remaining small-chain
overhead; it does not claim uniformly faster performance.

## Bounds and scope

- The generated matrix group is enumerated exactly. Default `--max-order` is
  256; exceeding it is an error, including when the group is infinite. This is
  not an algorithm for arbitrary huge finite groups.
- Rational central projectors are obtained by FLINT factorization. Simple
  models are constructed using subgroup fixed-space projectors, with at most
  1,024 subgroups and 64 attempts to separate the center. Character norms
  certify Schur-index-one models and a quaternionic Schur-index-two case.
  Some finite groups require a more general model constructor; those are
  rejected explicitly. The kernel engine itself is independent of this
  construction strategy and can be extended with a GAP-generated model
  adapter.
- The supplied defining matrices determine the represented group (its image).
  They cannot reveal additional elements acting trivially on that space.
  A terminal or other representation must satisfy the relations of this image.
- Public tests cover C3/C5/C7, D3/D5/D7, D7 × C2, S4, C2 × C2 and Q8, dense rational
  conjugations, repeated solves, tensor products, zero kernels, unconstrained
  kernels, vector terminals, empty recursive spaces, continuation and rejected
  non-invariant constraints.
- The archive benchmark covers FEC and **MHV** LEC. It does not claim an NMHV
  archive comparison, sewing integration, GUI integration, or high-weight
  production performance.
